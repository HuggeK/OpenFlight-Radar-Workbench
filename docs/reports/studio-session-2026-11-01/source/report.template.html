OpenFlight field report · 2026-11-01 · indoor studio · IWR6843 self-trigger

Studio Session 2026-11-01

OpenFlight on the IWR6843 self-trigger, hitting into a net beside a TrackMan 4. 127 IWR triggers over four runs, matched shot by shot against 76 TrackMan shots exported with SwingSync.

TL;DR

  • The OPS243 dropped from 30 to 20 kS/s at 11:37:53 and read 1.49 × TrackMan's ball speed until a restart at 12:35. Its own trigger offset went from 102 to 154 ms and its J3 window from 34 to 48 ms; UART noise sending S2 is the leading suspect. Outside that period, ball speed matched TrackMan to 0.995 ± 0.028.
  • The self-trigger caught 57 of 76 real shots on time (75 %). Six fired on the takeaway or backswing, ten never fired, three fired 60-70 ms too early.
  • False triggers are frequent and SNR does not fix them. Addresses, waggles, practice swings, placing a ball and walking up all fired it at SNR 1.5, 2.0 and 3.0; each one blinds the IWR for 7.7 s and can cost the next real shot.
  • IWR launch was 2.4° MAE (n=8), three times the project's 0.83° TrackMan baseline. --iwr6843-net-m must be unit → net; entering ball → net (3.0 m) clipped every track.
  • Real-time scheduling buys nothing: S! reaches the OPS in 0.17 ± 0.01 ms either way.

Lessons learned

  1. The OPS changed its sampling mid-run. At 11:37:53, with no host command in between, ball speed went to 1.486 ± 0.035 × TrackMan, club speed to 1.37 ×, the J3 post-trigger window from ~32 to ~48 ms, the log's post-trigger from 34.5 to 0.0 ms, and the clock sync from 36 to 59 valid readings. The OPS's own trigger offset in every capture went from exactly 102.00 to exactly 154.00 ms: 3072 pre-trigger samples at 30 kS/s, then at 20 kS/s. It was not a reboot (the OPS powers up at 30 kS/s in continuous mode) and not the move (that came at ~11:52). The OPS reported UART receive errors (4 noise, 4 framing, 1 break since boot); one candidate is a re-arm S#24 that lost its # and read as S2 (20 kS/s), to be confirmed against OmniPreSense's API. A restart fixed it.
  2. --iwr6843-net-m is unit → net. 3.0 m (meant as ball → net) left the tracker 0.5 m, about 10 ms of flight. Replaying the same dumps with 4.6 m tracked 24-41 ms on every real shot. Unit → net was 5.30 m.
  3. The self-trigger caught 75 % of real shots on time. Early triggers on the takeaway left the OPS reading the slow backswing (20-50 mph) and the real shot fell in the blind window.
  4. False triggers are frequent; SNR does not fix them. 20 saved shots and 41 no-ball triggers had no TrackMan shot behind them. At SNR 3.0 a practice swing still triggered and real shots went missing.
  5. Every trigger blinds the IWR for ~7.7 s with --debug: 7.1 s readback plus a radar restart.
  6. IWR launch angles were 3× worse than the baseline: 2.4° MAE, 1.4° median, +1.5° bias on 8 clean shots, with the radar 0.04 m above the floor, lower than any validated mount (0.1524 m). Replaying 31 shots with the ball centre at its real 0.061 m (4 cm mat + 2.1 cm radius) instead of 0.04 m cut the bias on the 23 good tracks from +0.87° to +0.39°, but 8 shots stayed 5-11° off: wrong tracks, not geometry, dominate the error.
  7. Measure both heights from the floor the unit stands on. Radar height to the centre of the IWR antenna group, ball height to the centre of the ball (mat + 0.021 m).
  8. The clocks were fine. NTP (systemd-timesyncd) syncs whenever there is a connection, and the RTC battery charges and kept time across power-off. TrackMan's clock, not the Pi's, was 425.5 s off.
  9. Real-time scheduling buys nothing. The window jitter that looked halved was the 11:37:53 switch.
  10. “TI radar capture failed” is misleading. It shows on every shot because the firmware never has an onboard result ready; the data is still saved.
  11. Launch OpenFlight from the desktop icon. Over SSH with nohup … & the server ignored SIGINT, and SIGTERM skipped the OPS idle step.

Against TrackMan

TrackMan's clock ran 425.5 s ahead of the Pi's NTP time; at that offset 66 of its 76 shots line up with an IWR trigger within 1 s. TrackMan stamps a shot 70 ± 29 ms after OpenFlight's trigger, so a trigger more than 0.3 s before TrackMan's stamp is an early one.

On the right side the IWR matched the project's 0.83° baseline; beside the TrackMan it got worse, with more wrong tracks. The trigger and the OPS behaved the same in both places. The alcove wall next to the unit (extra reflections) and the aim are the first suspects; the samples are small, so treat it as a lead. Positions should not be mixed when comparing IWR results.
